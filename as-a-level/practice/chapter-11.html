Section A: Paper 1 style

Choose one answer for each question. Then press Mark my answers.

  1. What is an antigen?

    Answer: D. Antigens are usually proteins or glycoproteins.

  2. What does 'self' mean in immunology?

    Answer: B. Lymphocytes that react to self are removed during development.

  3. Which cells carry out phagocytosis?

    Answer: B. Both are phagocytes.

  4. What happens after a phagocyte engulfs a bacterium?

    Answer: A. Hydrolytic enzymes digest it.

  5. What is the role of a macrophage in a primary immune response?

    Answer: D. Antigen presentation activates T helper cells.

  6. What is the role of memory cells?

    Answer: B. They give long-term immunity.

  7. What is clonal selection?

    Answer: D. Only lymphocytes specific to the antigen are activated.

  8. Which cells secrete antibodies?

    Answer: D. Plasma cells develop from B lymphocytes.

  9. What is the role of T helper cells?

    Answer: A. They coordinate the response.

  10. What do T killer cells do?

    Answer: C. They destroy cells showing foreign antigens.

  11. Why is the secondary response faster and larger than the primary response?

    Answer: A. Memory cells are already present in large numbers.

  12. Which part of an antibody binds to the antigen?

    Answer: C. Variable regions have a shape complementary to the antigen.

  13. How many polypeptide chains make up one antibody molecule?

    Answer: D. Two heavy and two light chains.

  14. Which is an example of natural passive immunity?

    Answer: B. Antibodies received, not made.

  15. Which is an example of artificial active immunity?

    Answer: C. The body makes its own antibodies and memory cells.

  16. Why does passive immunity not give long-term protection?

    Answer: C. No immune response by the person.

  17. What is herd immunity?

    Answer: C. It protects unvaccinated people.

  18. How are hybridoma cells made in the production of monoclonal antibodies?

    Answer: A. Hybridomas divide indefinitely and make one antibody.

  19. Which is a use of monoclonal antibodies?

    Answer: C. Used in diagnosis and treatment.

  20. Why does a vaccine provide long-term immunity?

    Answer: B. Memory cells give a fast secondary response.

Section B: Paper 2 style

Write your answers in your exercise book, then check against the mark scheme.

Question 1 [8 marks]

  1. Describe how a neutrophil destroys a bacterium. [4]
  2. Explain the difference between the primary and secondary immune responses. [4]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) chemotaxis / attracted to bacteria; receptors bind to bacterium; engulfs by endocytosis / phagocytosis; phagocytic vacuole forms; lysosomes fuse; hydrolytic enzymes digest bacterium [max 4]
  • (b) primary: first exposure, slow, few specific lymphocytes, clonal selection and expansion takes time, low antibody level, symptoms; secondary: memory cells present, recognise antigen quickly, rapid division, faster and larger antibody production, pathogen destroyed before symptoms [max 4]

Question 2 [9 marks]

  1. Describe the roles of B lymphocytes and T lymphocytes in the immune response. [6]
  2. Draw and label the structure of an antibody. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) B cells: specific B cell with complementary receptor activated / clonal selection; divides by mitosis (clonal expansion); plasma cells secrete antibodies; memory B cells; T helper cells: release cytokines, stimulate B cells and killer T cells; T killer cells: attach to and kill infected body cells; memory T cells [max 6]
  • (b) Y shape: two heavy and two light chains; variable regions / antigen-binding sites at tips; constant region; disulfide bonds; hinge region [max 3]

Question 3 [8 marks]

  1. Distinguish between active and passive immunity, giving one natural and one artificial example of each. [4]
  2. Explain how vaccination programmes can help to control the spread of an infectious disease. [4]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) active: own immune response, antibodies and memory cells made, long-lasting; natural: infection; artificial: vaccination; passive: antibodies received, no memory cells, short-term; natural: placenta / breast milk; artificial: antibody / antitoxin injection [max 4]
  • (b) vaccine contains antigens / weakened or inactivated virus; stimulates primary response and memory cells; secondary response on infection prevents disease; high coverage gives herd immunity; transmission chain broken; works best when the pathogen's antigens do not change [max 4]

Question 4 [7 marks]

  1. Outline how monoclonal antibodies are produced. [4]
  2. Explain how monoclonal antibodies can be used in diagnosis or treatment. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) inject mouse with antigen; B cells / plasma cells producing the antibody taken from spleen; fused with myeloma (cancer) cells; hybridoma cells; selected and cloned; secrete one type of antibody [max 4]
  • (b) antibody binds specifically to one antigen; diagnosis: e.g. pregnancy test detecting hCG / detecting a pathogen, often with dye or label; treatment: e.g. binds to cancer cell antigens, blocking receptors or marking cells for destruction / delivering drugs [max 3]
