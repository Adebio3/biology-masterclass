Section A: Paper 1 style

Choose one answer for each question. Then press Mark my answers.

  1. A student heats a solution with Benedict's solution and it stays blue. She then boils a fresh sample with dilute hydrochloric acid, neutralises it and repeats the test. An orange-red precipitate forms. What does the solution contain?

    Answer: C. Negative first test, positive after acid hydrolysis: a non-reducing sugar such as sucrose was hydrolysed to reducing sugars.

  2. Which test result shows that a solution contains protein?

    Answer: D. Biuret reagent turns lilac/purple with peptide bonds.

  3. How do α-glucose and β-glucose differ?

    Answer: C. The –OH on carbon 1 is below the ring in α-glucose and above it in β-glucose.

  4. Which pair of molecules forms sucrose?

    Answer: B. Sucrose is a disaccharide of glucose and fructose.

  5. Which reaction forms a glycosidic bond?

    Answer: A. Monosaccharides join by condensation, removing a water molecule.

  6. Which feature of amylopectin is NOT found in amylose?

    Answer: A. Amylopectin has 1,6 branch points; amylose is unbranched.

  7. Why is glycogen well suited as an energy store in animals?

    Answer: D. Many branches give many ends for enzymes to remove glucose rapidly.

  8. What holds neighbouring cellulose molecules together in a microfibril?

    Answer: D. Many hydrogen bonds form between –OH groups of parallel chains.

  9. Why does every other β-glucose in cellulose appear rotated by 180°?

    Answer: A. The –OH on carbon 1 is above the ring, so alternate molecules are inverted to form 1,4 bonds, giving straight chains.

  10. How many water molecules are released when one triglyceride is made?

    Answer: C. One glycerol + three fatty acids: three ester bonds, three water molecules.

  11. What makes a fatty acid unsaturated?

    Answer: C. Unsaturated fatty acids contain one or more C=C double bonds, causing kinks.

  12. Which statement about phospholipids is correct?

    Answer: B. One fatty acid of a triglyceride is replaced by a polar phosphate group.

  13. Which groups react to form a peptide bond?

    Answer: B. Condensation between –COOH and –NH₂, releasing water.

  14. Which level of protein structure is held only by peptide bonds?

    Answer: A. Primary structure is the amino acid sequence.

  15. Which bonds hold an α-helix in shape?

    Answer: C. Secondary structure is held by hydrogen bonds in the backbone.

  16. Which bond in a protein is a covalent bond between two cysteine R groups?

    Answer: C. Disulfide bridges are strong covalent bonds.

  17. Why is haemoglobin soluble in water?

    Answer: D. Globular proteins fold with hydrophilic R groups outside.

  18. Which feature of collagen gives it high tensile strength?

    Answer: A. Cross-linked, staggered triple helices have no weak points.

  19. Why does water have a high specific heat capacity?

    Answer: C. Energy goes into breaking hydrogen bonds, so temperature rises slowly.

  20. Which property of water makes sweating an effective way to cool the body?

    Answer: A. Evaporation takes a lot of energy from the skin.

Section B: Paper 2 style

Write your answers in your exercise book, then check against the mark scheme.

Question 1 [9 marks]

A student has four unlabelled solutions: glucose, sucrose, starch and egg albumen (a protein).

  1. Describe how the student could use the biuret test to identify the protein. [2]
  2. Explain how the student could distinguish between glucose and sucrose. [4]
  3. Describe how the Benedict's test could be made semi-quantitative to compare the glucose concentration of two solutions. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) add biuret reagent / sodium hydroxide then dilute copper(II) sulfate; lilac / purple colour shows protein (blue = negative) [2]
  • (b) Benedict's test on both: heat in a water bath (about 80 °C); glucose gives a green/yellow/orange/brick-red precipitate; sucrose stays blue (non-reducing); then boil sucrose with dilute HCl, neutralise with NaHCO₃, retest: positive [max 4]
  • (c) same volume of each solution and of Benedict's; same temperature and heating time; compare colour with standards of known concentration / time the first colour change / filter and weigh precipitate / use a colorimeter [max 3]

Question 2 [9 marks]

  1. Draw the ring structure of α-glucose. [2]
  2. Describe how two α-glucose molecules are joined to form maltose. [3]
  3. Explain how the structure of starch makes it suitable as a storage molecule in plants. [4]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) six-membered ring with O between C1 and C5; correct –H and –OH positions with –OH on C1 below the ring and CH₂OH on C5 above [2]
  • (b) condensation reaction; between –OH on C1 of one and –OH on C4 of the other; water removed; 1,4 glycosidic bond [max 3]
  • (c) insoluble: does not affect water potential / does not leave cells; amylose helix is compact; branched amylopectin gives many ends for rapid hydrolysis; made of α-glucose, easily hydrolysed to glucose for respiration; large molecule [max 4]

Question 3 [9 marks]

  1. Describe the structure of a triglyceride. [3]
  2. Explain how the structure of triglycerides makes them suitable as energy stores. [3]
  3. Explain why phospholipids, but not triglycerides, form bilayers in water. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) one glycerol; three fatty acids; joined by ester bonds; formed by condensation; fatty acids may be saturated or unsaturated [max 3]
  • (b) many C–H bonds / high proportion of hydrogen; release more than twice as much energy per gram as carbohydrate when oxidised; insoluble so no effect on water potential; compact; also metabolic water [max 3]
  • (c) phospholipid has a phosphate group in place of one fatty acid; phosphate head is polar / hydrophilic and faces water; fatty acid tails are non-polar / hydrophobic and point inwards away from water; triglyceride is entirely non-polar [max 3]

Question 4 [9 marks]

  1. Describe the primary, secondary and tertiary structure of a protein. [5]
  2. Haemoglobin and collagen are both proteins. Compare the structure of haemoglobin with the structure of collagen. [4]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) primary: sequence of amino acids joined by peptide bonds; secondary: α-helix or β-pleated sheet; held by hydrogen bonds between C=O and N–H of backbone; tertiary: further folding into 3D shape; held by hydrogen, ionic, disulfide bonds and hydrophobic interactions between R groups [max 5]
  • (b) both have quaternary structure / more than one polypeptide; haemoglobin: globular, four chains (2α, 2β), haem with Fe²⁺, soluble; collagen: fibrous, three chains in a triple helix, glycine every third amino acid, insoluble, cross-links between molecules [max 4]
