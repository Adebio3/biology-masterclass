Section A: Multiple choice

Choose one answer for each question. Then press Mark my answers.

  1. Photosynthesis is an energy transfer process in which…

    Answer: C. Light energy is used to make ATP and reduced NADP, then sugars.

  2. Where does the light-dependent stage take place?

    Answer: C. Photosystems and ETC are in thylakoid membranes.

  3. Where does the Calvin cycle take place?

    Answer: C. Rubisco and Calvin cycle enzymes are in the stroma.

  4. Which pigments are found in chloroplasts?

    Answer: B. Carotenoids are accessory pigments.

  5. What does an absorption spectrum show?

    Answer: C. The action spectrum shows rate of photosynthesis.

  6. A pigment moves 30 mm and the solvent front 75 mm. Rf =

    Answer: A. Rf = 30 ÷ 75 = 0.4.

  7. Which product of photolysis is released as a waste product?

    Answer: D. Oxygen diffuses out of the leaf.

  8. Which photosystems are used in cyclic photophosphorylation?

    Answer: D. Electrons return to PSI.

  9. Which products of non-cyclic photophosphorylation are used in the Calvin cycle?

    Answer: D. They are needed to reduce GP to TP.

  10. How is ATP made in the chloroplast?

    Answer: A. Chemiosmosis, as in mitochondria.

  11. Which enzyme fixes carbon dioxide?

    Answer: B. Rubisco catalyses the combination of CO₂ and RuBP.

  12. What is the first product of carbon fixation?

    Answer: C. 6C intermediate splits into two 3C GP.

  13. What happens to most TP in the Calvin cycle?

    Answer: D. 5 of every 6 TP regenerate RuBP.

  14. If light is switched off, what happens to GP and RuBP concentrations?

    Answer: A. No ATP and reduced NADP to reduce GP or regenerate RuBP.

  15. If CO₂ is removed, what happens to RuBP?

    Answer: D. RuBP is not used in fixation but is still made.

  16. A limiting factor is…

    Answer: B. Increasing it increases the rate.

  17. On a graph of rate against light intensity, the plateau shows…

    Answer: B. Light is no longer the limiting factor.

  18. In the pondweed experiment, light intensity is varied by…

    Answer: A. Inverse square law.

  19. DCPIP is used to investigate…

    Answer: A. DCPIP accepts electrons and changes from blue to colourless.

  20. Why are crop plants in glasshouses sometimes given extra CO₂?

    Answer: A. Increasing CO₂ increases the rate of photosynthesis when light and temperature are adequate.

Section B: Paper 4 style

Write your answers in your exercise book, then check against the mark scheme.

Question 1 [6 marks]

  1. Describe how chloroplast structure is related to its function. [4]
  2. Explain the difference between an absorption spectrum and an action spectrum. [2]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) thylakoid membranes with photosystems: large surface area for light absorption; grana stacks; ATP synthase in membrane; stroma contains Calvin cycle enzymes / rubisco; starch grains / lipid droplets; DNA and ribosomes [max 4]
  • (b) absorption: light absorbed by pigment at each wavelength; action: rate of photosynthesis at each wavelength [2]

Question 2 [8 marks]

  1. Describe non-cyclic photophosphorylation. [6]
  2. State two differences between cyclic and non-cyclic photophosphorylation. [2]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) light absorbed by PSII; electrons excited; passed to ETC; photolysis of water replaces electrons; O₂ released; protons pumped into thylakoid space; ATP made by chemiosmosis / ATP synthase; PSI excited; electrons reduce NADP [max 6]
  • (b) cyclic: PSI only vs both; no photolysis vs photolysis; no reduced NADP vs reduced NADP; no O₂ vs O₂ [max 2]

Question 3 [8 marks]

  1. Outline the Calvin cycle. [5]
  2. Explain the effect on GP of switching off the light. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) CO₂ combines with RuBP; rubisco; two GP; GP reduced to TP using ATP and reduced NADP; some TP to make glucose / sugars; RuBP regenerated using ATP [max 5]
  • (b) GP rises; no ATP / reduced NADP from light-dependent stage; GP not reduced to TP; GP still formed until RuBP used up [max 3]

Question 4 [8 marks]

  1. Describe how you could use paper chromatography to separate leaf pigments. [4]
  2. Explain why light intensity, CO₂ concentration and temperature are limiting factors. [4]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) grind leaves with propanone; concentrated spot on pencil origin line; place in solvent below origin; remove when solvent near top; mark front; calculate Rf values [max 4]
  • (b) light: energy for light-dependent stage / ATP and reduced NADP; CO₂: substrate for rubisco / fixation; temperature: affects enzyme activity in Calvin cycle; factor in shortest supply limits rate [max 4]
