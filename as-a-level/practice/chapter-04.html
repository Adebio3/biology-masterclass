Section A: Paper 1 style

Choose one answer for each question. Then press Mark my answers.

  1. Why is the cell surface membrane described as a fluid mosaic?

    Answer: A. Fluid = components move; mosaic = proteins scattered.

  2. Which part of the phospholipid bilayer is hydrophobic?

    Answer: D. Non-polar fatty acid tails face inwards.

  3. What is the main role of cholesterol in the membrane?

    Answer: D. It stops the membrane becoming too fluid or too rigid.

  4. Which components act as receptors and in cell recognition?

    Answer: A. Carbohydrate chains form specific shapes on the outer surface.

  5. In cell signalling, why does a hormone affect only its target cells?

    Answer: A. The ligand binds a specific receptor.

  6. Which molecule can diffuse directly through the phospholipid bilayer?

    Answer: A. Small, non-polar molecules pass through the bilayer.

  7. Which is true of facilitated diffusion?

    Answer: B. Passive transport through specific proteins.

  8. Which change would increase the rate of diffusion across an exchange surface?

    Answer: C. Rate depends on gradient, area, distance and temperature.

  9. What is the surface area to volume ratio of a cube with sides of 3 cm?

    Answer: B. SA = 54 cm², V = 27 cm³, ratio 2 : 1.

  10. Agar cubes of side 1 cm, 2 cm and 3 cm are placed in acid. Which statement is correct?

    Answer: B. Same diffusion distance per unit time; the small cube has the shortest distance to its centre.

  11. What is the water potential of pure water at atmospheric pressure?

    Answer: B. Pure water has the highest possible water potential, 0 kPa.

  12. Cell X has ψ = −500 kPa and cell Y has ψ = −800 kPa. Which statement is correct?

    Answer: C. Water moves from higher (less negative) to lower water potential.

  13. A red blood cell is placed in distilled water. What happens?

    Answer: C. Water enters by osmosis; there is no cell wall.

  14. Why does a plant cell not burst when placed in distilled water?

    Answer: A. The wall exerts pressure, making the cell turgid.

  15. In a plasmolysed plant cell, what fills the space between the cell wall and the cell surface membrane?

    Answer: D. The cell wall is fully permeable.

  16. Potato cylinders show no change in mass in 0.25 mol dm⁻³ sucrose (ψ = −680 kPa). What is the water potential of the potato cells?

    Answer: B. No net movement: ψ cell = ψ solution.

  17. Mineral ions enter root hair cells from soil water with a lower ion concentration. This is…

    Answer: C. Movement against the gradient, using ATP.

  18. A respiratory poison stops ATP production. Which process stops?

    Answer: C. Only active transport needs ATP from respiration.

  19. How does a phagocyte take in a bacterium?

    Answer: C. The membrane engulfs it, forming a vesicle.

  20. How are digestive enzymes released from pancreatic cells?

    Answer: A. Secretory vesicles fuse with the cell surface membrane.

Section B: Paper 2 style

Write your answers in your exercise book, then check against the mark scheme.

Question 1 [10 marks]

  1. Describe the fluid mosaic model of membrane structure. [4]
  2. State the roles of cholesterol, glycoproteins and channel proteins in the cell surface membrane. [3]
  3. Outline how a hormone causes a response in a target cell. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) phospholipid bilayer; hydrophilic heads outward, hydrophobic tails inward; fluid: phospholipids and proteins move sideways; mosaic: proteins scattered; intrinsic proteins span the bilayer, extrinsic on surface; cholesterol between phospholipids; glycoproteins/glycolipids on outer surface [max 4]
  • (b) cholesterol: regulates fluidity / stability; glycoproteins: receptors / cell recognition / antigens; channel proteins: water-filled pore for specific ions / polar molecules (facilitated diffusion) [3]
  • (c) hormone (ligand) binds to complementary receptor on cell surface membrane; receptor changes shape; triggers a chain of reactions inside the cell / second messenger; specific response [max 3]

Question 3 [11 marks]

  1. Define water potential and osmosis. [2]
  2. Explain what happens to a red blood cell and to a plant cell placed in pure water. [4]
  3. Describe how you would use potato tissue to estimate its water potential. [5]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) ψ: tendency of water to move from one place to another (kPa); osmosis: net movement of water from higher to lower water potential across a partially permeable membrane [2]
  • (b) pure water has higher ψ than cells; water enters by osmosis; red blood cell swells and bursts (lysis) as it has no wall; plant cell becomes turgid; cellulose wall resists expansion / pressure potential; does not burst [max 4]
  • (c) range of sucrose concentrations; cut cylinders of equal size; blot and weigh; leave in solutions for same time, same temperature; blot and reweigh; calculate % change in mass; plot against concentration; concentration at 0 % change; convert to ψ using a table [max 5]

Question 4 [7 marks]

  1. Compare active transport with facilitated diffusion. [4]
  2. Describe how a large particle such as a bacterium is taken into a phagocyte. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) both use specific transport proteins; facilitated diffusion: down concentration gradient, passive / no ATP, channel or carrier; active transport: against gradient, needs ATP from respiration, carrier proteins only (pumps) that change shape [max 4]
  • (b) endocytosis / phagocytosis; membrane extends around bacterium; fuses to form a vesicle / phagocytic vacuole; requires ATP [max 3]
