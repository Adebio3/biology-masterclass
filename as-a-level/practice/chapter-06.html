Section A: Paper 1 style

Choose one answer for each question. Then press Mark my answers.

  1. Which three components make up a nucleotide?

    Answer: D. A nucleotide = phosphate + pentose + base.

  2. Which bases are purines?

    Answer: D. Purines have a double ring.

  3. ATP is best described as…

    Answer: B. Adenine + ribose + three phosphates.

  4. What type of bond joins nucleotides in one strand of DNA?

    Answer: B. Phosphodiester bonds form the sugar–phosphate backbone.

  5. How many hydrogen bonds form between guanine and cytosine?

    Answer: C. G–C: three; A–T: two.

  6. A DNA sample contains 22% thymine. What percentage is cytosine?

    Answer: B. A = T = 22%; G + C = 56%; C = 28%.

  7. What does 'antiparallel' mean for DNA?

    Answer: B. One strand runs 5′→3′, the other 3′→5′.

  8. Which is a difference between DNA and mRNA?

    Answer: D. mRNA: single strand, ribose, uracil.

  9. Why is DNA replication described as semi-conservative?

    Answer: C. Each parental strand acts as a template.

  10. What is the role of DNA ligase in replication?

    Answer: C. It forms phosphodiester bonds between fragments.

  11. Why is the lagging strand made in short fragments?

    Answer: B. Polymerase must work away from the fork on that strand.

  12. What is a gene?

    Answer: A. Genes code for polypeptides.

  13. The genetic code is degenerate. This means…

    Answer: A. 64 codons, 20 amino acids.

  14. A DNA template strand reads TAC CGA. What is the mRNA?

    Answer: A. Complementary, with U instead of T.

  15. Which enzyme makes mRNA during transcription?

    Answer: A. RNA polymerase joins RNA nucleotides.

  16. What happens to introns in eukaryotic cells?

    Answer: C. Introns are spliced out; exons joined.

  17. An mRNA codon is GCU. What is the anticodon on the tRNA?

    Answer: D. Complementary: G–C, C–G, U–A.

  18. How many bases in mRNA code for a polypeptide of 120 amino acids, excluding the stop codon?

    Answer: D. 3 bases per amino acid.

  19. Which mutation is most likely to cause a frameshift?

    Answer: C. One-base insertions or deletions shift the reading frame.

  20. In sickle cell anaemia, which amino acid replaces glutamic acid in the β-globin chain?

    Answer: A. A base substitution changes GAG to GUG in mRNA.

Section B: Paper 2 style

Write your answers in your exercise book, then check against the mark scheme.

Question 1 [8 marks]

  1. Describe the structure of a DNA molecule. [5]
  2. State three differences between the structure of DNA and mRNA. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) two polynucleotide strands; double helix; sugar–phosphate backbone / phosphodiester bonds; antiparallel; bases on the inside; complementary base pairing A–T, C–G; hydrogen bonds (2 for A–T, 3 for C–G); purine pairs with pyrimidine [max 5]
  • (b) DNA double-stranded, mRNA single; deoxyribose vs ribose; thymine vs uracil; DNA much longer; DNA has hydrogen bonds between strands [max 3]

Question 2 [8 marks]

  1. Describe the semi-conservative replication of DNA. [5]
  2. Explain why replication of the two strands differs (leading and lagging strands). [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) hydrogen bonds between bases break; strands separate; both strands act as templates; free nucleotides pair by complementary base pairing; DNA polymerase joins nucleotides / forms phosphodiester bonds; each new molecule has one old and one new strand [max 5]
  • (b) DNA polymerase only adds nucleotides 5′ → 3′; strands are antiparallel; leading strand made continuously towards the fork; lagging strand made in short fragments away from the fork; DNA ligase joins fragments [max 3]

Question 3 [10 marks]

  1. Describe how a gene is transcribed and the transcript processed in a eukaryotic cell. [5]
  2. Describe the roles of mRNA, tRNA and ribosomes in translation. [5]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) DNA unwinds at the gene / hydrogen bonds break; one strand is the template; free RNA nucleotides pair complementarily (U with A); RNA polymerase joins nucleotides; primary transcript; introns removed, exons joined; mRNA leaves via nuclear pore [max 5]
  • (b) mRNA attaches to ribosome; codons read; tRNA carries specific amino acid; anticodon complementary to codon; two tRNAs at a time in ribosome; peptide bond forms between amino acids; ribosome moves along one codon; until stop codon [max 5]

Question 4 [6 marks]

Part of the coding strand of a gene is: ATG GTG CAC CTG ACT CCT GAG.

  1. Write the mRNA transcribed from this gene. [1]
  2. The last codon changes from GAG to GTG. Name this type of mutation and explain its effect on the protein. [3]
  3. Explain why deleting the first G of the second codon would have a larger effect. [2]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) AUG GUG CAC CUG ACU CCU GAG [1]
  • (b) substitution; changes one codon so one amino acid changes (glutamic acid → valine); may change the protein's tertiary structure / function, as in sickle cell haemoglobin [max 3]
  • (c) frameshift; every codon after the deletion changes / many amino acids changed / may create early stop codon [2]
