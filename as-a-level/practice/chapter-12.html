Section A: Multiple choice

Choose one answer for each question. Then press Mark my answers.

  1. Why do organisms need energy?

    Answer: B. Energy is needed for work: active transport, synthesis, movement and maintaining body temperature in mammals and birds.

  2. Which describes ATP?

    Answer: B. ATP contains adenine, ribose and three phosphate groups.

  3. Why is ATP a suitable energy currency?

    Answer: B. Small packets of energy reduce waste; ATP is soluble and easily transported in cells.

  4. Where does glycolysis take place?

    Answer: C. Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm.

  5. What is the net gain of ATP from glycolysis per glucose?

    Answer: A. 4 made, 2 used: net 2.

  6. What happens in the link reaction?

    Answer: C. Carbon dioxide and reduced NAD are produced.

  7. How many CO₂ molecules are released per turn of the Krebs cycle?

    Answer: A. Two decarboxylations per turn.

  8. Which coenzyme carries hydrogen from the Krebs cycle?

    Answer: C. Reduced NAD and reduced FAD carry H to the ETC.

  9. What is substrate-level phosphorylation?

    Answer: D. Some ATP is made directly, not by chemiosmosis.

  10. What is the final electron acceptor in aerobic respiration?

    Answer: D. Oxygen combines with electrons and protons to form water.

  11. Where are protons pumped during oxidative phosphorylation?

    Answer: C. This builds a proton gradient across the inner membrane.

  12. How is ATP made by chemiosmosis?

    Answer: D. Proton flow down the gradient drives ATP synthesis.

  13. What is the advantage of the folded cristae?

    Answer: D. More ETC proteins means more ATP.

  14. In anaerobic respiration in mammals, pyruvate is converted to…

    Answer: B. Lactate dehydrogenase uses reduced NAD.

  15. Why is the conversion of pyruvate to lactate important?

    Answer: A. Without NAD, glycolysis stops.

  16. Which respiratory substrate has the highest energy value per gram?

    Answer: B. Lipids contain more hydrogen atoms per molecule.

  17. RQ = 0.7 suggests the respiratory substrate is…

    Answer: A. RQ: carbohydrate 1.0, protein about 0.9, lipid about 0.7.

  18. In a respirometer, the potassium hydroxide (soda lime)…

    Answer: D. So the movement shows oxygen uptake.

  19. Rice can grow with its roots in water because…

    Answer: A. Rice also respires anaerobically, producing ethanol.

  20. Redox indicator DCPIP or methylene blue can be used to investigate respiration in yeast because…

    Answer: C. Faster decolourisation means a faster rate of respiration.

Question 1 [6 marks]

  1. Describe the structure of ATP. [3]
  2. Explain why ATP is described as the universal energy currency. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) adenine; ribose; three phosphate groups; phosphorylated nucleotide [max 3]
  • (b) used in all organisms; hydrolysed in one step; releases small, manageable amount of energy; soluble / easily transported; quickly regenerated [max 3]

Question 2 [7 marks]

  1. Outline the link reaction and the Krebs cycle. [6]
  2. State where in the mitochondrion each takes place. [1]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) pyruvate enters mitochondrion; decarboxylated and dehydrogenated; acetyl CoA formed; acetyl (2C) combines with oxaloacetate (4C) to form citrate (6C); decarboxylation and dehydrogenation; reduced NAD and reduced FAD formed; ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation; oxaloacetate regenerated [max 6]
  • (b) both in the matrix [1]

Question 3 [9 marks]

  1. Explain how ATP is produced by oxidative phosphorylation. [6]
  2. Explain why less ATP is made when oxygen is absent. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) reduced NAD / FAD give H to ETC; H splits into protons and electrons; electrons passed along carriers; energy released pumps protons into intermembrane space; proton gradient; protons diffuse back through ATP synthase; ADP + Pi → ATP; oxygen final electron acceptor; water formed [max 6]
  • (b) no final electron acceptor; ETC stops; NAD not regenerated in mitochondria; only glycolysis; net 2 ATP [max 3]

Question 4 [8 marks]

  1. Describe how a respirometer could be used to measure the rate of oxygen uptake by germinating seeds. [5]
  2. Explain how the respiratory quotient (RQ) can show which substrate is being respired. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) seeds in tube with KOH / soda lime to absorb CO₂; control tube with glass beads; water bath for constant temperature; coloured liquid moves towards seeds as O₂ taken up; measure distance in set time; volume = πr²h; repeat [max 5]
  • (b) RQ = CO₂ produced ÷ O₂ used; carbohydrate 1.0; lipid 0.7; protein 0.9 [max 3]
