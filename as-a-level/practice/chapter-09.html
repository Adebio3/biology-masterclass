Section A: Paper 1 style

Choose one answer for each question. Then press Mark my answers.

  1. What is the correct order in which air passes through the gas exchange system?

    Answer: B. Airways branch into smaller tubes.

  2. What is the function of cartilage in the trachea?

    Answer: B. C-shaped rings stop collapse when pressure falls.

  3. Why is the cartilage in the trachea C-shaped rather than a complete ring?

    Answer: C. The gap lets the oesophagus expand when swallowing.

  4. Which cells secrete mucus in the airways?

    Answer: A. Goblet cells (and mucous glands) secrete mucus.

  5. What is the role of cilia in the trachea?

    Answer: D. The mucus escalator.

  6. Which tissue is found in the walls of bronchioles but not in the alveoli?

    Answer: B. Smooth muscle controls airway diameter.

  7. Why do the smallest bronchioles have no cartilage?

    Answer: A. Muscle can adjust their diameter.

  8. What is the role of elastic fibres in the alveoli?

    Answer: C. They stretch during inspiration and recoil to help expel air.

  9. What type of epithelium lines the alveoli?

    Answer: A. Thin, flattened cells for a short diffusion distance.

  10. By what process does oxygen move from the alveoli into the blood?

    Answer: C. Down a concentration gradient.

  11. Which feature does NOT help gas exchange in the alveoli?

    Answer: C. Cartilage would increase diffusion distance.

  12. How is a steep concentration gradient for oxygen maintained at the alveoli?

    Answer: C. Fresh air in; oxygenated blood carried away.

  13. Approximately how many cells thick is the barrier between the air in an alveolus and the blood?

    Answer: B. Alveolar epithelium plus capillary endothelium.

  14. What is the function of smooth muscle in the bronchi and bronchioles?

    Answer: A. Controls airflow.

  15. Where are mucous glands found?

    Answer: D. Glands in the submucosa add mucus.

  16. Which statement about carbon dioxide at the alveolus is correct?

    Answer: D. Higher concentration in blood than in alveolar air.

  17. Which of these is lined with ciliated epithelium?

    Answer: D. Ciliated epithelium lines the trachea and bronchi.

  18. Why are alveoli surrounded by many capillaries?

    Answer: D. Extensive capillary network.

  19. What happens to the elastic fibres in the alveolar walls during inspiration?

    Answer: B. They recoil during expiration.

  20. Which tissue in the trachea traps dust and microorganisms?

    Answer: A. Particles stick to the mucus.

Section B: Paper 2 style

Write your answers in your exercise book, then check against the mark scheme.

Question 1 [8 marks]

  1. Describe the distribution of cartilage, ciliated epithelium, goblet cells and smooth muscle in the trachea, bronchi and bronchioles. [4]
  2. Explain how the trachea is protected from infection by inhaled microorganisms. [4]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) cartilage: C-shaped rings in trachea, irregular plates in bronchi, none in bronchioles; ciliated epithelium and goblet cells in trachea and bronchi (fewer / absent in smallest bronchioles); smooth muscle in trachea, bronchi and bronchioles (relatively more in bronchioles) [max 4]
  • (b) goblet cells / mucous glands secrete mucus; microorganisms / dust trapped; cilia beat; move mucus up to the throat; swallowed / removed; (macrophages engulf any reaching alveoli) [max 4]

Question 2 [7 marks]

  1. Describe how the alveoli are adapted for efficient gas exchange. [5]
  2. Explain the role of elastic fibres in the walls of the alveoli. [2]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) very large number giving large surface area; squamous epithelium one cell thick; capillary wall one cell thick, so short diffusion distance; dense capillary network / good blood supply maintains gradient; ventilation maintains gradient; moist lining for gases to dissolve [max 5]
  • (b) stretch during inspiration; recoil during expiration to help expel air / prevent alveoli bursting [2]

Question 3 [8 marks]

  1. Name: (i) the tissue that keeps the trachea open; (ii) the tissue that changes the diameter of the bronchioles; (iii) the cells that secrete mucus. [3]
  2. Explain why the bronchioles do not need cartilage. [2]
  3. Explain why oxygen diffuses into the blood at the alveoli and carbon dioxide diffuses out. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) (i) cartilage; (ii) smooth muscle; (iii) goblet cells [3]
  • (b) narrow so do not collapse; walls supported by elastic tissue; need to change diameter (smooth muscle) [max 2]
  • (c) concentration of O₂ higher in alveolar air than blood; CO₂ higher in blood than air; gradients maintained by ventilation and by blood flow; diffusion across thin walls [max 3]

Question 4 [6 marks]

Compare the trachea with an alveolus in terms of the type of epithelium, the presence of cartilage and their main function. [6]

Show mark scheme
  • trachea: ciliated epithelium with goblet cells; alveolus: squamous epithelium; trachea has C-shaped cartilage, alveolus none; trachea: conducts air, keeps open, removes particles; alveolus: site of gas exchange; alveolus has elastic fibres and is surrounded by capillaries; trachea has smooth muscle and mucous glands [max 6]
