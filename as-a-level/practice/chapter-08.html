Section A: Paper 1 style

Choose one answer for each question. Then press Mark my answers.

  1. Why is the mammalian circulation described as double?

    Answer: B. Pulmonary and systemic circuits.

  2. Which blood vessel carries deoxygenated blood?

    Answer: D. The pulmonary artery carries blood from the right ventricle to the lungs.

  3. Why do arteries have thick walls with elastic fibres?

    Answer: A. Elastic recoil smooths the pressure.

  4. Why do veins contain valves?

    Answer: C. Blood pressure in veins is low.

  5. Which feature makes capillaries suited to exchange?

    Answer: C. Short diffusion distance.

  6. How is tissue fluid formed?

    Answer: B. Hydrostatic pressure exceeds the water potential gradient at the arterial end.

  7. Why does most tissue fluid return to the capillary at the venous end?

    Answer: C. Lower hydrostatic pressure; plasma proteins lower ψ.

  8. Which component of blood is NOT found in tissue fluid?

    Answer: A. Large proteins cannot leave capillaries.

  9. Which cell type has a lobed nucleus and engulfs bacteria?

    Answer: D. Neutrophils are phagocytes with lobed nuclei.

  10. Which feature of red blood cells increases oxygen uptake?

    Answer: D. Also short diffusion distance; no nucleus leaves more room for haemoglobin.

  11. What is the chloride shift?

    Answer: B. It keeps the red blood cell electrically neutral.

  12. What does the Bohr shift describe?

    Answer: B. The curve moves to the right.

  13. What is the role of carbonic anhydrase in red blood cells?

    Answer: A. Carbonic acid then dissociates to H⁺ and HCO₃⁻.

  14. What forms when H⁺ ions combine with haemoglobin?

    Answer: A. This helps release oxygen and buffers pH.

  15. Why is the wall of the left ventricle thicker than the right?

    Answer: B. The systemic circuit needs higher pressure.

  16. What causes the atrioventricular valves to close?

    Answer: D. This prevents backflow into the atria.

  17. Why is cardiac muscle described as myogenic?

    Answer: D. Excitation starts in the SAN.

  18. Where does each heartbeat start?

    Answer: A. The SAN is the pacemaker.

  19. Why is there a short delay at the atrioventricular node?

    Answer: B. Ensures ventricles fill.

  20. What is the role of Purkyne tissue?

    Answer: D. Contraction from the apex pushes blood up into the arteries.

Section B: Paper 2 style

Write your answers in your exercise book, then check against the mark scheme.

Question 1 [7 marks]

  1. Compare the structure of an artery with the structure of a vein. [4]
  2. Explain how the structure of capillaries is related to their function. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) artery: thicker wall; more elastic fibres; more smooth muscle; narrower lumen relative to wall; no valves (except at heart); vein: thinner wall, wide lumen, valves [max 4]
  • (b) wall one cell thick / endothelium only: short diffusion distance; narrow lumen: red cells close to wall, slow flow; gaps between cells: allow tissue fluid formation / phagocytes to leave; large network / surface area [max 3]

Question 2 [7 marks]

  1. Explain how tissue fluid is formed and how most of it is returned to the blood. [5]
  2. State two differences between blood plasma and tissue fluid. [2]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) at arterial end hydrostatic pressure high; greater than water potential gradient effect; plasma (water, glucose, ions, oxygen) forced out through gaps in capillary wall; proteins too large to leave; at venous end hydrostatic pressure lower; plasma has lower ψ due to proteins; water returns by osmosis; rest drains into lymph [max 5]
  • (b) tissue fluid has fewer / no plasma proteins; no red blood cells; fewer white cells; less oxygen / glucose, more CO₂ [max 2]

Question 3 [10 marks]

  1. Describe the role of haemoglobin in transporting oxygen. [3]
  2. Explain the Bohr shift and its importance in respiring tissues. [4]
  3. Describe how carbon dioxide is transported in the blood. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) four haem groups each with Fe²⁺; each binds one O₂; oxyhaemoglobin formed in lungs at high pO₂; releases O₂ at low pO₂ in tissues; cooperative binding / S-shaped curve [max 3]
  • (b) high CO₂ in respiring tissues; carbonic anhydrase forms carbonic acid → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻; H⁺ binds to haemoglobin forming haemoglobinic acid; reduces affinity for oxygen; dissociation curve shifts right; more oxygen released where needed [max 4]
  • (c) most as hydrogencarbonate ions in plasma (made in red cells via carbonic anhydrase, diffuse out); some combined with haemoglobin as carbaminohaemoglobin; some dissolved in plasma [3]

Question 4 [7 marks]

  1. Describe how the cardiac cycle is initiated and coordinated. [5]
  2. During ventricular systole, explain why the semilunar valves open. [2]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) heart muscle is myogenic; SAN (pacemaker) in right atrium sends wave of excitation over atria; atria contract; non-conducting tissue between atria and ventricles; AVN delays the impulse; allows atria to empty / ventricles to fill; impulse passes down Purkyne tissue (bundle of His) in septum to apex; ventricles contract from base upwards [max 5]
  • (b) ventricular pressure rises above pressure in aorta / pulmonary artery; pushes valves open [2]
