Section A: Paper 1 style

Choose one answer for each question. Then press Mark my answers.

  1. What are chromosomes made of?

    Answer: D. DNA wound around histones forms chromatin.

  2. Which structure holds two sister chromatids together?

    Answer: C. The centromere joins sister chromatids.

  3. Sister chromatids are…

    Answer: B. Made in S phase by semi-conservative replication.

  4. What are telomeres?

    Answer: C. Telomeres protect the ends of chromosomes.

  5. Which is NOT a role of mitosis?

    Answer: C. Mitosis gives genetically identical cells.

  6. In which phase of the cell cycle is DNA replicated?

    Answer: A. S = synthesis phase.

  7. A cell in G1 contains 8 units of DNA. How much DNA does it contain in G2?

    Answer: C. DNA doubles in S phase.

  8. Which stage takes up most of the cell cycle?

    Answer: A. Mitosis and cytokinesis are short.

  9. In which stage of mitosis does the nuclear envelope break down?

    Answer: A. It breaks down at the end of prophase.

  10. In which stage are chromosomes lined up on the equator of the spindle?

    Answer: B. Spindle fibres attach at centromeres.

  11. What happens during anaphase?

    Answer: D. Spindle fibres shorten.

  12. What happens during telophase?

    Answer: C. Chromosomes also decondense.

  13. Which structures are absent from dividing plant cells?

    Answer: A. Plant cells form a spindle without centrioles.

  14. How is cytokinesis different in plant cells?

    Answer: D. Vesicles form a cell plate which becomes the new wall.

  15. A cell with 46 chromosomes divides by mitosis. How many chromosomes does each daughter cell contain?

    Answer: B. Mitosis maintains chromosome number.

  16. Of 250 cells in a root tip squash, 30 are in mitosis. What is the mitotic index?

    Answer: B. 30 ÷ 250 = 0.12.

  17. Why are root tips used to observe mitosis?

    Answer: B. Meristems have a high mitotic index.

  18. Why do telomeres allow cells to divide many times without losing genes?

    Answer: D. DNA cannot be fully copied at the very end; telomeres are lost instead.

  19. What is a stem cell?

    Answer: B. Stem cells replace and repair tissues.

  20. How can uncontrolled cell division lead to a tumour?

    Answer: C. A mass of cells forms.

Section B: Paper 2 style

Write your answers in your exercise book, then check against the mark scheme.

Question 1 [9 marks]

  1. Describe the structure of a chromosome at metaphase. [4]
  2. Explain the importance of telomeres. [3]
  3. Outline the role of stem cells in tissue repair. [2]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) two sister chromatids; joined at centromere; each chromatid one DNA molecule; DNA wound around histones; condensed / coiled; telomeres at ends [max 4]
  • (b) repeated non-coding sequences at ends of chromosomes; DNA shortens at each replication; telomere lost instead of genes; allows continued replication / prevents loss of genes [max 3]
  • (c) stem cells divide by mitosis; daughter cells differentiate to replace damaged / lost cells [2]

Question 2 [8 marks]

  1. Name the stages of the mitotic cell cycle in order, starting at G1. [2]
  2. Describe what happens to the mass of DNA in a cell during one cell cycle. [3]
  3. Explain why the daughter cells produced by mitosis are genetically identical. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) G1, S, G2, (prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase), cytokinesis [2]
  • (b) constant in G1; doubles during S phase (DNA replication); stays doubled through G2 and mitosis; halves at cytokinesis [max 3]
  • (c) DNA replicated semi-conservatively / complementary base pairing; sister chromatids identical; chromatids separated so each daughter gets one copy of each chromosome; same number of chromosomes [max 3]

Question 3 [8 marks]

  1. Describe the behaviour of chromosomes, the nuclear envelope and the spindle during mitosis in an animal cell. [6]
  2. State two differences between mitosis in plant cells and animal cells. [2]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) prophase: chromosomes condense, visible as two chromatids; nuclear envelope breaks down; centrioles move to poles, spindle forms; metaphase: chromosomes line up at equator; spindle fibres attach to centromeres; anaphase: centromeres divide, chromatids pulled to opposite poles as spindle fibres shorten; telophase: chromosomes decondense; nuclear envelope re-forms [max 6]
  • (b) plants: no centrioles; cell plate forms in cytokinesis (animals: cleavage furrow); in plants mainly at meristems [max 2]
