Section A: Paper 1 style

Choose one answer for each question. Then press Mark my answers.

  1. Which pathogen causes cholera?

    Answer: A. Cholera is a bacterial disease.

  2. How is cholera usually transmitted?

    Answer: A. Faecal–oral transmission.

  3. Which measure best prevents the spread of cholera?

    Answer: D. Breaks faecal–oral transmission.

  4. Which organism causes malaria?

    Answer: A. Plasmodium is a protoctist.

  5. How is malaria transmitted?

    Answer: C. The mosquito is the vector.

  6. Which is a method of controlling malaria?

    Answer: A. Prevents mosquito bites at night.

  7. Which pathogens cause tuberculosis?

    Answer: C. Both bacteria cause TB.

  8. How is TB transmitted?

    Answer: D. Overcrowding increases spread.

  9. What type of pathogen is HIV?

    Answer: B. HIV has RNA and reverse transcriptase.

  10. Which cells does HIV infect and destroy?

    Answer: A. Loss of helper T cells weakens the immune system.

  11. Which is a way HIV is transmitted?

    Answer: B. Also unprotected sex and from mother to baby.

  12. Which measure helps to control the spread of TB?

    Answer: D. TB control combines vaccination, early diagnosis and full drug courses.

  13. Which measure reduces the transmission of HIV?

    Answer: D. These stop exchange of body fluids.

  14. Which is a social or economic factor that makes TB harder to control?

    Answer: C. Crowding increases airborne transmission; poverty limits treatment.

  15. How does penicillin kill bacteria?

    Answer: C. Weakened walls cannot resist osmotic pressure in growing bacteria.

  16. Why are antibiotics ineffective against viruses?

    Answer: B. Antibiotics target bacterial structures and processes.

  17. How does antibiotic resistance arise in a bacterial population?

    Answer: A. Selection of a pre-existing mutation.

  18. How can resistance genes spread between bacteria?

    Answer: C. Horizontal gene transfer.

  19. Which practice helps reduce antibiotic resistance?

    Answer: B. Reduces selection for resistant bacteria.

  20. Which disease is caused by a pathogen that is transmitted by a vector?

    Answer: C. The Anopheles mosquito is the vector.

Section B: Paper 2 style

Write your answers in your exercise book, then check against the mark scheme.

Question 1 [7 marks]

  1. For cholera, state the causative organism and describe how it is transmitted. [3]
  2. Explain why cholera is more common in areas without clean water and sanitation. [2]
  3. Suggest two ways to reduce the spread of cholera after a flood. [2]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) Vibrio cholerae; bacterium; in water / food contaminated with faeces of infected people; faecal–oral route [max 3]
  • (b) sewage contaminates drinking water; untreated water not chlorinated / boiled [2]
  • (c) boil or chlorinate drinking water; proper sewage disposal / latrines; hygiene (hand washing); cook food well; oral vaccine [max 2]

Question 2 [7 marks]

  1. Describe how malaria is transmitted. [3]
  2. Explain why malaria is difficult to control. [4]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) Plasmodium (protoctist); female Anopheles mosquito is the vector; takes blood meal from infected person; parasites develop in mosquito; injected into another person when mosquito feeds [max 3]
  • (b) mosquitoes breed in small areas of standing water / hard to remove all; mosquitoes resistant to insecticides; Plasmodium resistant to drugs; parasite inside red cells / liver, hidden from immune system; antigens change; vaccines only partly effective; cost / poverty / lack of infrastructure [max 4]

Question 3 [7 marks]

  1. Describe how HIV is transmitted and explain how it causes AIDS. [5]
  2. Explain why TB is common in people with HIV. [2]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) in body fluids: unprotected sex; sharing needles; blood transfusion; mother to child across placenta / at birth / breast milk; HIV infects helper T lymphocytes; number of helper T cells falls; immune system weakened; opportunistic infections, e.g. TB, pneumonia = AIDS [max 5]
  • (b) weakened immune system / few helper T cells; latent TB becomes active / cannot fight Mycobacterium [2]

Question 4 [9 marks]

  1. Explain how penicillin kills bacteria. [3]
  2. Explain how a population of bacteria becomes resistant to an antibiotic. [4]
  3. Suggest two ways to reduce the spread of antibiotic resistance. [2]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) inhibits enzymes that form cross-links in peptidoglycan; in growing / dividing bacteria; wall weakened; water enters by osmosis; cell bursts [max 3]
  • (b) random mutation gives resistance (e.g. enzyme that breaks down penicillin); antibiotic kills non-resistant bacteria; resistant bacteria survive (selection); reproduce and pass on allele; resistance genes on plasmids passed to other bacteria [max 4]
  • (c) complete the course; only prescribe when needed / not for viral infections; avoid routine use in farming; use combinations / change antibiotics; infection control in hospitals [max 2]
