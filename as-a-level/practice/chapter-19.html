Section A: Multiple choice

Choose one answer for each question. Then press Mark my answers.

  1. Recombinant DNA is…

    Answer: A. Often from two species.

  2. Reverse transcriptase is used to…

    Answer: A. From retroviruses.

  3. Restriction endonucleases…

    Answer: A. Recognition sites are often palindromic.

  4. DNA ligase…

    Answer: D. Seals the sugar–phosphate backbone.

  5. Plasmids are used as vectors because…

    Answer: C. Recombinant plasmids replicate in bacteria.

  6. A promoter may be transferred with a gene so that…

    Answer: B. The host may not recognise the original promoter.

  7. Cells with a GFP marker gene…

    Answer: C. Easy identification.

  8. Gene editing involves…

    Answer: D. A form of genetic engineering.

  9. In PCR, DNA is denatured at…

    Answer: C. Hydrogen bonds break.

  10. Primers in PCR…

    Answer: A. Annealing at about 55 °C.

  11. Starting with 1 molecule, after 10 cycles of PCR there are…

    Answer: A. 2¹⁰.

  12. In gel electrophoresis DNA moves…

    Answer: A. Phosphate groups carry negative charge.

  13. Small fragments in a gel…

    Answer: B. The gel acts as a sieve.

  14. Microarrays can be used to…

    Answer: B. Labelled cDNA hybridises with probes.

  15. A benefit of genome databases is…

    Answer: A. Free sharing of data.

  16. Recombinant human insulin is preferred because…

    Answer: B. Also large, reliable supply.

  17. BRCA1 and BRCA2 testing…

    Answer: D. Allows informed choices.

  18. Gene therapy for SCID adds a functional allele for…

    Answer: B. To stem cells from bone marrow.

  19. GM salmon…

    Answer: C. Growth hormone gene and promoter from other fish.

  20. Bt cotton…

    Answer: D. Gene from Bacillus thuringiensis.

Section B: Paper 4 style

Write your answers in your exercise book, then check against the mark scheme.

Question 1 [8 marks]

  1. Explain the roles of restriction enzymes, DNA ligase and plasmids in producing recombinant bacteria. [6]
  2. Explain why reverse transcriptase is used when transferring a human gene into bacteria. [2]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) restriction enzyme cuts gene out at specific sites; same enzyme cuts plasmid; complementary sticky ends; ligase forms phosphodiester bonds; plasmid is vector; taken up by bacteria; replicated [max 6]
  • (b) makes cDNA from mRNA; no introns, which bacteria cannot remove [2]

Question 2 [7 marks]

  1. Describe the steps of PCR. [5]
  2. Explain why Taq polymerase is used. [2]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) mix DNA, primers, nucleotides, Taq; 95 °C strands separate; about 55 °C primers anneal; 72 °C Taq extends primers; cycle repeated; DNA doubles each cycle [max 5]
  • (b) from thermophilic bacteria; thermostable / not denatured at 95 °C; optimum 72 °C [max 2]

Question 3 [8 marks]

  1. Explain how gel electrophoresis separates DNA fragments. [4]
  2. Outline how microarrays are used to study gene expression. [4]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) wells at cathode; current; DNA negatively charged; moves to anode; smaller fragments move faster / further; compare with ladder [max 4]
  • (b) mRNA from two samples; converted to cDNA; labelled with different fluorescent dyes; hybridise to probes on slide; scanned; colour shows which genes expressed [max 4]

Question 4 [7 marks]

  1. Outline the advantages of genetic screening, using cystic fibrosis as an example. [3]
  2. Discuss the ethical and social issues of growing herbicide-resistant soybean. [4]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) identify carriers; informed family planning; embryo testing; early diagnosis of newborns and treatment [max 3]
  • (b) higher yield; easier weed control; gene transfer to weeds / superweeds; more herbicide use; farmers depend on companies; consumer concerns; labelling [max 4]
