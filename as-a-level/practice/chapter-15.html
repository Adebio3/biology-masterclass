Section A: Multiple choice

Choose one answer for each question. Then press Mark my answers.

  1. Compared with the nervous system, the endocrine system…

    Answer: C. Hormones may affect many target cells.

  2. Where is the cell body of a sensory neurone?

    Answer: C. Sensory neurones carry impulses to the CNS.

  3. Which cells make myelin?

    Answer: B. Myelin insulates the axon.

  4. A chemoreceptor in a taste bud depolarises when…

    Answer: C. Salt provides Na⁺ that enters through channels.

  5. The resting potential is about…

    Answer: A. Inside negative relative to outside.

  6. Which helps maintain the resting potential?

    Answer: A. 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in; K⁺ diffuses out.

  7. Depolarisation is caused by…

    Answer: D. Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open.

  8. Repolarisation is caused by…

    Answer: D. Voltage-gated K⁺ channels open.

  9. The refractory period…

    Answer: C. Na⁺ channels cannot reopen immediately.

  10. A stronger stimulus causes…

    Answer: B. All-or-nothing law.

  11. Saltatory conduction occurs because…

    Answer: B. Myelin insulates the rest of the axon.

  12. What causes vesicles to release acetylcholine?

    Answer: A. Ca²⁺ triggers exocytosis.

  13. Acetylcholinesterase…

    Answer: D. Prevents continuous stimulation.

  14. At a neuromuscular junction, depolarisation spreads into the fibre along…

    Answer: A. Transverse system tubules.

  15. Ca²⁺ is released from the…

    Answer: D. It binds to troponin.

  16. When Ca²⁺ binds to troponin…

    Answer: B. Cross-bridges can now form.

  17. Which band shortens during contraction?

    Answer: C. The H zone also shortens.

  18. ATP is needed in muscle contraction to…

    Answer: B. Without ATP, rigor occurs.

  19. A Venus fly trap closes when…

    Answer: A. Two action potentials are needed.

  20. Auxin causes cell elongation by…

    Answer: A. Acid growth hypothesis.

Question 2 [8 marks]

  1. Explain how an action potential is generated and how the resting potential is restored. [6]
  2. Explain the importance of the refractory period. [2]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) stimulus; Na⁺ channels open; Na⁺ in; threshold; more channels open; depolarisation to +30 mV; Na⁺ channels close; K⁺ channels open; K⁺ out; repolarisation; hyperpolarisation; Na⁺/K⁺ pump restores [max 6]
  • (b) one-way transmission; discrete impulses; limits frequency [max 2]

Question 3 [8 marks]

  1. Describe transmission across a cholinergic synapse. [6]
  2. Explain why synapses ensure one-way transmission. [2]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) action potential at presynaptic knob; Ca²⁺ channels open; Ca²⁺ in; vesicles fuse; ACh released by exocytosis; diffuses across cleft; binds receptors; Na⁺ channels open; depolarisation; acetylcholinesterase hydrolyses ACh [max 6]
  • (b) vesicles / ACh only in presynaptic knob; receptors only on postsynaptic membrane [2]

  • (b) embryo makes gibberellin; acts on aleurone layer; DELLA broken down / amylase gene transcribed; amylase hydrolyses starch to maltose for embryo [max 3]
