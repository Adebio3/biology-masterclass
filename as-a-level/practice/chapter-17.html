Section A: Multiple choice

Choose one answer for each question. Then press Mark my answers.

  1. Which shows continuous variation?

    Answer: C. A range of values between extremes.

  2. Discontinuous variation is usually controlled by…

    Answer: B. Distinct categories.

  3. The t-test compares…

    Answer: B. Degrees of freedom = n₁ + n₂ − 2.

  4. A t value is greater than the critical value at p = 0.05. This means…

    Answer: C. Reject the null hypothesis.

  5. Natural selection occurs because…

    Answer: A. The struggle for existence.

  6. Stabilising selection…

    Answer: A. Occurs in stable environments.

  7. Directional selection occurs when…

    Answer: D. E.g. antibiotic resistance.

  8. Genetic drift is most important in…

    Answer: C. Chance has a bigger effect.

  9. The founder effect…

    Answer: D. Allele frequencies may differ from the original population.

  10. A bottleneck…

    Answer: C. Some alleles may be lost.

  11. Antibiotics cause the frequency of resistance alleles to increase because…

    Answer: C. Antibiotics are a selection pressure.

  12. If 16% of a population show a recessive phenotype, q =

    Answer: A. q = √0.16.

  13. If q = 0.2, the frequency of carriers (2pq) is…

    Answer: B. 2 × 0.8 × 0.2.

  14. Which is NOT a condition for Hardy–Weinberg?

    Answer: A. There must be no selection.

  15. In selective breeding…

    Answer: A. Artificial selection.

  16. Inbreeding of maize produces…

    Answer: B. Hybrids are vigorous.

  17. Bulls for dairy herds are selected by…

    Answer: D. Progeny testing.

  18. Comparing DNA sequences between species can show…

    Answer: B. Fewer differences: more recent common ancestor.

  19. Allopatric speciation requires…

    Answer: D. No gene flow between the separated populations.

  20. Sympatric speciation can occur through…

    Answer: D. E.g. different breeding times or courtship.

Write your answers in your exercise book, then check against the mark scheme.

Question 1 [6 marks]

  1. Explain the genetic basis of continuous variation. [3]
  2. Explain how the t-test is used to compare two samples. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) many genes / polygenes; each has small additive effect; environment also affects; range of phenotypes [max 3]
  • (b) null hypothesis no significant difference; calculate t; df = n₁ + n₂ − 2; compare with critical value at p = 0.05; t greater: significant [max 3]

Question 2 [9 marks]

  1. Explain how natural selection can cause directional change in a population. [5]
  2. Outline how bacteria become resistant to antibiotics. [4]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) variation; environment changes; selection pressure; individuals with one extreme phenotype more likely to survive; reproduce; pass on alleles; allele frequency changes; mean shifts [max 5]
  • (b) random mutation; resistance allele; antibiotic kills susceptible; resistant survive and reproduce by binary fission; vertical / horizontal (plasmid) transmission [max 4]

Question 3 [6 marks]

In a population, 1 in 400 people have a recessive condition.
  1. Calculate the frequency of heterozygous carriers. [3]
  2. State three conditions for the Hardy–Weinberg principle. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) q² = 0.0025; q = 0.05; p = 0.95; 2pq = 0.095 [3]
  • (b) large population; random mating; no mutation; no migration; no selection [max 3]

Question 4 [9 marks]

  1. Describe how disease resistance has been introduced into wheat by selective breeding. [4]
  2. Explain how allopatric speciation occurs. [5]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) resistant variety crossed with high-yield variety; offspring with both features selected; bred together; repeated over many generations; test for resistance [max 4]
  • (b) geographical barrier; no gene flow; different mutations; different selection pressures; drift; allele frequencies change; reproductive isolation; cannot produce fertile offspring [max 5]
