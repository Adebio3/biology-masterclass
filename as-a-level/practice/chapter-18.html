Section A: Multiple choice

Choose one answer for each question. Then press Mark my answers.

  1. The biological species concept defines a species as…

    Answer: A. Reproductive isolation from other groups.

  2. A problem with the biological species concept is that it cannot be used for…

    Answer: B. Interbreeding cannot be tested.

  3. Which are the three domains?

    Answer: C. Based on rRNA and other evidence.

  4. Archaea differ from Bacteria in…

    Answer: D. Both are prokaryotes.

  5. Which is in the correct order?

    Answer: B. Largest to smallest.

  6. Fungi are…

    Answer: A. They feed saprotrophically or parasitically.

  7. Viruses are classified by…

    Answer: A. DNA or RNA, ss or ds.

  8. A niche is…

    Answer: B. Includes how it obtains energy and interacts.

  9. Biodiversity includes…

    Answer: B. Three levels.

  10. Quadrats should be placed randomly to…

    Answer: C. Use random coordinates.

  11. A belt transect is used to…

    Answer: C. Quadrats at intervals along a line.

  12. n₁ = 50, n₂ = 60, m₂ = 12. N =

    Answer: A. 50 × 60 ÷ 12.

  13. Mark–release–recapture assumes…

    Answer: D. Also marks do not harm animals.

  14. Spearman’s rank correlation is suitable for…

    Answer: A. Uses ranks.

  15. A correlation coefficient of +0.95 means…

    Answer: C. Correlation is not causation.

  16. Simpson’s index D = 0.9 indicates…

    Answer: B. D close to 1 is high.

  17. Which is a cause of extinction?

    Answer: D. Also climate change, competition and hunting.

  18. Seed banks store seeds…

    Answer: C. Reduces metabolism and damage.

  19. Embryo transfer into a surrogate allows…

    Answer: C. Surrogates may be a related common species.

  20. CITES…

    Answer: D. The IUCN publishes the Red List.

Section B: Paper 4 style

Write your answers in your exercise book, then check against the mark scheme.

Question 1 [7 marks]

  1. Describe the three-domain classification. [3]
  2. Outline features of the kingdoms Fungi and Plantae. [4]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) Archaea, Bacteria, Eukarya; Archaea and Bacteria prokaryotes; differ in membrane lipids / rRNA / cell walls; Eukarya have nucleus [max 3]
  • (b) Fungi: heterotrophic; chitin walls; hyphae; spores; Plantae: photosynthetic; chloroplasts; cellulose walls; multicellular [max 4]

Question 2 [7 marks]

  1. Describe how you would use a belt transect to investigate the effect of light intensity on plant distribution. [5]
  2. State why Spearman’s rank correlation might be used to analyse the results. [2]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) tape across habitat; quadrats at regular intervals; record species / percentage cover; light meter reading at each quadrat; repeat transects; same time of day [max 5]
  • (b) to test for correlation between two variables; data may not be normally distributed / not linear [2]

Question 3 [5 marks]

  1. A sample of 40 snails was marked. Later, 50 were caught and 8 were marked. Estimate the population. [2]
  2. Explain why D for a habitat with 5 equally common species is higher than for one where one species makes up 90%. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) N = 40 × 50 ÷ 8 = 250 [2]
  • (b) D takes account of richness and evenness; dominance by one species gives large Σ(n/N)²; D lower; even abundance gives D close to 1 [3]

Question 4 [8 marks]

  1. Explain why species can become extinct. [4]
  2. Describe how IVF and embryo transfer help to conserve endangered mammals. [4]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) climate change: habitat changes faster than adaptation; competition from introduced species; hunting / poaching; habitat loss and degradation [max 4]
  • (b) hormones to stimulate many eggs; eggs collected; fertilised in vitro with fresh / frozen sperm; embryos transferred to surrogate; possibly related species; increases genetic diversity / number of offspring [max 4]
