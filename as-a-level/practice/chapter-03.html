Section A: Paper 1 style

Choose one answer for each question. Then press Mark my answers.

  1. Which statement describes an enzyme?

    Answer: B. Enzymes are globular proteins and are not used up.

  2. Which enzyme acts intracellularly?

    Answer: D. Catalase works inside cells; the others are secreted.

  3. What determines the shape of an enzyme's active site?

    Answer: D. Folding of the polypeptide gives the precise 3D shape.

  4. How does the induced-fit hypothesis differ from the lock-and-key hypothesis?

    Answer: A. In induced fit, binding causes the active site to mould around the substrate.

  5. How do enzymes increase the rate of reactions?

    Answer: A. They provide a pathway with lower activation energy.

  6. In an investigation of catalase, what is measured?

    Answer: C. 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂; oxygen is collected.

  7. Why is the initial rate used when comparing enzyme activity?

    Answer: C. Later, rate falls as substrate is used up.

  8. An amylase reaction takes 125 s to reach its end point. What is the rate?

    Answer: A. Rate = 1 ÷ 125 = 0.008 s⁻¹.

  9. Why does the rate of an enzyme reaction increase between 20 °C and 35 °C?

    Answer: C. More collisions per second, more E–S complexes.

  10. Which bonds are broken first when an enzyme is heated above its optimum?

    Answer: A. Weak hydrogen (and ionic) bonds break, changing tertiary structure.

  11. Why does a change in pH reduce enzyme activity?

    Answer: A. The active site shape changes.

  12. At high substrate concentration the rate levels off. Why?

    Answer: B. Enzyme concentration is then limiting.

  13. What is Vmax?

    Answer: B. Vmax is the theoretical maximum rate.

  14. What is Km?

    Answer: B. Km is the Michaelis–Menten constant.

  15. Enzyme P has Km = 1.5 mmol dm⁻³; enzyme Q has Km = 6 mmol dm⁻³ for the same substrate. Which is correct?

    Answer: A. Lower Km = higher affinity.

  16. How does a competitive inhibitor affect Vmax and Km?

    Answer: A. Enough substrate can out-compete it, so Vmax is reached, but more substrate is needed for ½Vmax.

  17. How does a non-competitive inhibitor affect Vmax and Km?

    Answer: C. Some enzyme is always inhibited, but working enzymes have normal affinity.

  18. Which describes a non-competitive inhibitor?

    Answer: C. It binds elsewhere on the enzyme.

  19. How is an enzyme immobilised in alginate?

    Answer: D. Calcium alginate beads trap the enzyme.

  20. Which is an advantage of immobilised enzymes over enzymes in solution?

    Answer: C. Easy separation and reuse; often more stable too.

Section B: Paper 2 style

Write your answers in your exercise book, then check against the mark scheme.

Question 1 [9 marks]

  1. Explain how an enzyme catalyses a reaction, using the induced-fit hypothesis. [4]
  2. Explain why an enzyme is specific to one substrate. [2]
  3. Sketch and label a graph to show the energy changes in a reaction with and without an enzyme. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) substrate has complementary shape and binds to active site; enzyme–substrate complex; active site changes shape slightly to fit closely; puts strain on bonds / holds substrates close; lowers activation energy; products released, enzyme unchanged [max 4]
  • (b) active site has a specific shape (tertiary structure); only one substrate is complementary / can bind [2]
  • (c) axes: energy and progress of reaction; two curves from substrate level to products; enzyme curve has lower peak; activation energy labelled for each [3]

Question 2 [9 marks]

A student investigated the effect of hydrogen peroxide concentration on the activity of catalase from potato.

  1. Describe a method the student could use. [6]
  2. The student found the initial rates in the table.
    H₂O₂ concentration / %0.51248
    initial rate / cm³ min⁻¹2.13.96.48.18.4
    Explain the results. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) prepare at least five concentrations by dilution; same mass / number of discs of potato, same surface area; same volume of H₂O₂; constant temperature (water bath) and pH (buffer); collect O₂ in gas syringe / inverted measuring cylinder; record volume at intervals; tangent at t = 0 for initial rate; repeat and calculate mean [max 6]
  • (b) rate increases with concentration: more frequent successful collisions / more E–S complexes; at 4–8 % rate levels off; all active sites occupied / enzyme concentration limiting / close to Vmax [3]

Question 3 [9 marks]

  1. Explain the meaning of Vmax and Km. [2]
  2. Explain why Km is used to compare the affinity of enzymes for their substrates. [2]
  3. Compare the effects of a competitive and a non-competitive inhibitor on an enzyme-catalysed reaction. [5]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) Vmax: maximum rate when all active sites are saturated; Km: substrate concentration at which rate is half of Vmax [2]
  • (b) low Km means ½Vmax reached at low substrate concentration; so active site binds substrate readily / high affinity; independent of enzyme concentration [max 2]
  • (c) competitive: similar shape to substrate; binds to active site; competes; effect reduced by more substrate; Vmax unchanged, Km increased; non-competitive: binds at another site; changes active site shape; not overcome by more substrate; Vmax reduced, Km unchanged [max 5]

Question 4 [8 marks]

  1. Describe how lactase can be immobilised in alginate beads. [3]
  2. Milk is passed through a column of immobilised lactase. Suggest and explain the effect of increasing the flow rate on the amount of lactose hydrolysed. [2]
  3. Give three advantages of using immobilised enzymes. [3]
Show mark scheme
  • (a) mix lactase with sodium alginate; draw into syringe and drip into calcium chloride solution; beads of calcium alginate form trapping enzyme; rinse [max 3]
  • (b) less lactose hydrolysed; less contact time between lactose and active sites [2]
  • (c) enzyme easily recovered / reused; product not contaminated; more stable to temperature / pH changes; continuous process; easier to control [max 3]
